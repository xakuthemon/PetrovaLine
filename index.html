    Decision desk

    About the data. TerraWaltz is a teaching tool built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge “Dancing with the SARs”. Scenes, time series and figures are procedurally simulated and the geography is schematic. They illustrate how L-band and S-band radar respond to roughness, moisture, structure and motion. They are not NISAR products and must not be used for operational decisions.

    NISAR is a joint NASA–ISRO mission launched on 30 July 2025. It carries an L-band radar (24 cm) and an S-band radar (9.4 cm) and revisits land and ice every 12 days. Years before the launch are shown as an L-band analogue.

    Before the first dance

    Why Active Radar?

    A camera in orbit waits for sunlight to bounce off the ground. A radar brings its own light: microwave pulses centimetres long, which pass through cloud, smoke and darkness.

    Passive optical

    • Records reflected sunlight, 0.4–2.5 µm.
    • Blocked by cloud, smoke and haze.
    • Blind at night and through the polar winter.
    • Sees colour: the top surface only.

    Active microwave SAR

    • Sends its own pulse and times the echo.
    • Passes through cloud, smoke and light rain.
    • Works day and night, every pass.
    • Reads roughness, moisture, structure, and by phase, centimetres of motion.

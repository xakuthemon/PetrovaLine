The planet moves. NISAR measures by how much.

Two radars on one spacecraft image nearly all of Earth's land and ice every 12 days, through cloud, smoke and darkness. Each pass is compared with the last, down to centimetres.

Subsystem B · Radar

Two wavelengths, one aperture.

L-band waves are 24 cm long: they pass through forest canopy and hold their phase for years. S-band waves are 9.4 cm long: a finer ruler for small motion and light vegetation. Both feeds illuminate the same reflector.

The echo carries three things: how rough the ground is, how much water it holds, and, in its phase, how far it has moved since the last pass. Under the beam: the Caspian shore at Aktau and Lake Karakol.

    B

    What the echo carries

    Three quantities turn a returned pulse into a picture of the surface. The console lets you change each one and watch the pixel respond.

    σ⁰Surface roughness
    Smooth surfaces mirror the pulse away and look dark. Roughness near the wavelength scatters it back. Trunks, reeds and walls over smooth water return it twice as a double-bounce.
    εDielectric constant
    Water is near 80, dry sand and salt near 3. Wet ground reflects more. Liquid water in snow absorbs the pulse and the surface turns dark.
    φPhase
    Two passes compared give line-of-sight motion. One colour cycle is half a wavelength: 11.9 cm at L-band, 4.7 cm at S-band.

      Primer

      Why Active Radar?

      A camera in orbit waits for sunlight to bounce off the ground. A radar brings its own light: microwave pulses centimetres long, which pass through cloud, smoke and darkness.

      Passive optical

      • Records reflected sunlight, 0.4–2.5 µm.
      • Blocked by cloud, smoke and haze.
      • Blind at night and through the polar winter.
      • Sees colour: the top surface only.

      Active microwave SAR

      • Sends its own pulse and times the echo.
      • Passes through cloud, smoke and light rain.
      • Works day and night, every pass.
      • Reads roughness, moisture, structure, and by phase, centimetres of motion.
